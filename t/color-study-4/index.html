Color Study — No. 4

(Published on 21. October 2026 by Berdy) — draft, not yet on Logic Masters

A sudoku painted in color: no digits, just the wheel, the lines and a little mixing.

Play on SudokuPad →
Three gray cells in every row, column and box: gray is the one color that repeats.
Rules
Color sudoku
Shade every cell. Each row, column and 3×3 box contains red, orange, yellow, green, blue and purple once each, plus three gray cells.
Gray may repeat; there are no digits, so ordinary Sudoku rules do not apply. The pre-shaded cell keeps its color.
Color wheel
Red → orange → yellow → green → blue → purple → back to red. Gray is not on the wheel, so it never appears on a yellow or blue line.
On a line that closes into a loop, the step from its last cell back to its first follows the line's rule too.
Yellow lines (slow)
Choose one direction around the wheel for the whole line. Each step along the line either keeps the same color or moves one position in that direction.
Blue lines (constant step)
Every step along the line moves the same number of positions around the wheel, in the same direction. Each line has its own step size, which may be zero.
Mixing arrows
The colors in all the cells of a shaft mix to make the color in its circle. A mix is the set of primaries it contains.
Primaries: red, yellow and blue.
Mixes: orange = red + yellow, green = yellow + blue, purple = blue + red, gray = all three.
Repeats add nothing: red + red = red, red + orange = orange, orange + blue = gray, and anything mixed with gray is gray. Each shaft is mixed on its own.
Glows
A reddish-orange glow inside a cell's edge marks a warm cell (red, orange or yellow); a blue-gray glow marks a cool cell (green, blue or purple).
