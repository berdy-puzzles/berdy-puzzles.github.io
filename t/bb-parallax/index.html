Black Box One: Into the Void

By Berdy — Playtest version

Black Box is a pencil puzzle inspired by the work of Godfrey Hounsfield, who was awarded a Nobel Prize for his invention of the CAT scanner.

Play on SudokuPad →

5 matching pairs and 5 lone shooters. Colour and symbol identify the beam type; the shooter's shape identifies its partner.

Rules

Sudoku

Normal Sudoku rules apply.

Atoms

Exactly 7 cells hide an atom. Each atom's digit is smaller than every orthogonally adjacent digit.

↔

Beams

Each pair of matching shooters is joined by one beam. A shooter without a matching counterpart means its beam was absorbed by an atom. See illustrations below for full mechanics disambiguation.
Illustrated Black Box ray rules ↗

German whispers Lime green · zigzag

Neighboring digits along a beam differ by at least 5.

Renban Magenta · rising bars

Digits along a beam are distinct and consecutive, in any order.

Region sum Blue · equals sign

Box boundaries split a beam into segments with equal sums. Separate visits to a box count as separate segments.

Palindrome White · mirrored chevrons

Digits along a beam read the same from either end.

Zipper Orange · zipper teeth

A beam visits an odd number of cells. Digits equally far from the middle sum to the middle digit.

How beams travel

No atom: straight through.Atom straight ahead: absorbed.Straight ahead beats diagonal: still absorbed.Atom diagonally ahead: turns away.Atoms diagonally ahead on both sides: straight back.Atom at the edge: deflected before it enters.

The beam turns in the cell before the atom's diagonal. Every cell it passes through, in order, counts as a cell along the beam; a cell it turns in counts once.
